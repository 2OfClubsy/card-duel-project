# Week 2 Analysis

## Learning points

During Week 2, the team learned that user stories help separate the product idea into smaller and clearer requirements. This made it easier to understand which parts of the game are required for the first playable version and which parts can be postponed.

MoSCoW prioritization helped the team reduce the initial MVP v1 scope. Instead of trying to build a large card game immediately, the team focused on the most important parts: clear UI, interesting core mechanics, and understandable challenges.

The team also learned that the prototype and MVP v0 have different purposes. The prototype is used to discuss the planned player experience with the customer, while MVP v0 is a runnable technical foundation that proves the product can be opened and checked.

The customer meeting showed that presentation and atmosphere are important for this project. The customer explained that a mostly 3D format would support immersion better than a fully 2D format.

## Validated assumptions

The team validated that the core idea of a horror-inspired card duel game is acceptable to the customer.

The customer approved the documented user stories, MoSCoW priorities, and initial proposed MVP v1 scope.

The team also confirmed that the first playable version should stay small and focus on a simple card duel loop rather than a large amount of content.

The assumption that a separate demo version is important was rejected. Based on customer feedback, the previous demo-related story was replaced with a story about immersive 3D presentation.

## Needs clarification

The team still needs to clarify how complex the 3D environment should be for the first playable version.

The team also needs to decide which mechanics will be included in the first card duel loop and which mechanics should be postponed.

The final visual style, sound direction, and exact balance between 3D gameplay and 2D UI elements still need more discussion and testing.

Another open question is how much horror atmosphere can be created within the available course time without making the MVP v1 too complex.

## Planned response

The team will keep the initial MVP v1 scope small and focus on the user stories selected in `user-stories.md`.

The team will replace the old demo-related requirement with the updated US-06 about immersive 3D presentation.

The prototype and future MVP v1 work will focus on a mostly 3D game format, while 2D elements may still be used for cards, UI, menus, and overlays.

The team will continue by preparing the MVP v0 foundation and documenting a repeatable smoke-check scenario in `mvp-v0-report.md`.
